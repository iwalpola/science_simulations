REACTION BUILDER — tutorial speaker notes
=========================================
Roughly 11-12 minutes with the demo pauses. [SHOW] = do this on screen;
the rest is what you say.

Sections 3 and 4 carry a "word for word" block. That is an ALTERNATIVE to
the bullets above it, not an extra — the two cover the same ground, so read
one or the other. Bullets if you are comfortable ad-libbing, script if you
would rather not think about wording while you are also driving the mouse.


1. THE ONE IDEA  (30s)
----------------------
[SHOW] Empty board, arrow button in the middle.

- Single HTML file. No install, works offline.
- Left of the arrow: reactants. Right: products.
- The rule everything is built on: products are the SAME ATOMS, rearranged.
  You can never add or delete matter on the right, only convert it.
  That is why the two sides have different tools.


2. GETTING THINGS ON THE BOARD  (45s)
-------------------------------------
[SHOW] Type "sulfuric" in the search box.

- Search takes names, formulas, and categories: lithium, carbon dioxide,
  sulfuric acid, carbonate.
- Arrow keys to move, Enter to add — or drag a result straight onto the board.
- It always lands on the LEFT and is mirrored to the right automatically.
- Presets dropdown (bottom right) has 14 ready-made GCSE reactions.
  "Clear board" empties everything.

[SHOW] Load "Metal + acid (Mg + HCl)".


3. PIECES AND HOW THEY JOIN  (1.5-2 min)
----------------------------------------
[SHOW] Point at the tiles.

- Dashed outline = neutral atom. Solid = ion. Small dark tile = electron.
- Ions are jigsaw pieces: a positive ion has holes on its right, a negative
  ion has tabs on its left, one per unit of charge. Slide them together and
  they lock into one piece — that is why H+ and Cl- sit as a pair.
- Charge is not a number to remember, it is the shape of the tile.
- Two identical atoms placed side by side bond on their own: 2H becomes H2.
- Carbon is special: it is a block with two tabs each side, and oxygen has
  the matching holes. Plug an oxygen onto each side of a carbon — that is
  how you build CO2.


   ---- word for word, if you want it ----

   Every tile tells you what it is before you read the label. Dashed
   outline, neutral atom. Solid tile, ion. And the little dark ones are
   electrons.

   Now look at the edges. A positive ion has holes punched into its
   right-hand side. A negative ion has tabs sticking out on its left. One
   for each unit of charge — so a magnesium two-plus has two holes, and a
   chloride, one-minus, has a single tab.

   [slide Mg2+ and two Cl- together]

   Push them together and they lock. That's magnesium chloride — and
   notice I never typed a formula and never balanced anything. The shapes
   only fit one way. That's the whole idea: charge stops being a number to
   remember and becomes something you can see.

   [break an H2 apart, leave the two atoms side by side]

   Atoms behave differently. Put two identical atoms next to each other —
   hydrogen, oxygen, chlorine, that family — and they bond on their own.
   Two H's become H-two.

   [bring in a carbon and two oxygens]

   And then there's carbon, which has a shape of its own. It's a block
   with two tabs on each side, because carbon holds four bonds. Oxygen has
   the matching holes. So: oxygen on the left, oxygen on the right — and
   that is carbon dioxide, waiting to be made. Hold that thought.


4. THE PRODUCTS-SIDE TOOLS  (3.5-4 min)  ** the core of the video **
--------------------------------------------------------------------
[SHOW] Hover (or tap) a piece on the right. Buttons appear beside it.

Go through these on real pieces rather than listing them:

  Scissors    Break the bonds / Split apart.
              Splits a molecule into atoms, or an ionic compound into ions.
              On a CH4 tile it breaks it into its atoms: C + 4H.

  Undo arrow  Turn into its ion.
              An atom gives up electrons (Na -> Na+ + e-) or takes them in
              (Cl + e- -> Cl-). This is oxidation and reduction.

  Scissors    Take the electrons off — an anion back to an atom.

  Plus        Make a neutral atom.
              A positive ion plus the right number of electrons. If the
              electrons are loose elsewhere on the board it will fly them in.

  Purple      Decomposition, e.g. CO3(2-) -> O(2-) + CO2. Only appears on
              ions that actually do this.

  Droplet     Combine into H2O / CO2.
              Appears when the piece you have built adds up to a molecule:
              H+ with OH-, or 2H+ with O(2-), or oxygen either side of carbon.

Key point to land: **reduction uses real electrons.** If none are loose you
get an error dialog. Electrons come from somewhere.

[SHOW] Reactants side by contrast: only Duplicate and Delete.


   ---- word for word, if you want it ----

   Everything you do to the products happens on the piece itself. Hover
   over one — or tap it, on a touchscreen — and the buttons appear beside
   it.

   [hover an ionic pair]

   Scissors splits things up. On an ionic compound it says "split apart"
   and hands you the separate ions. On a molecule it says "break the
   bonds" and hands you loose atoms.

   [hover a CH4 tile]

   Methane is drawn as a single tile, so there are no bonds to cut — but
   the scissors still open it, into a carbon and four hydrogens.

   [hover a sodium atom]

   This next one is the important one. The curved arrow turns an atom into
   its ion. Sodium gives up an electron — and there it is, on the board,
   as its own tile. That's oxidation, and the log has already written the
   half-equation for you.

   [hover a chlorine atom]

   The same button on a chlorine goes the other way: chlorine takes an
   electron in. And here is the bit I really want you to notice — it needs
   a real one. If there is no free electron anywhere on the board, it
   refuses, and tells you why. Electrons have to come from somewhere.
   That's not the software being fussy, that's the chemistry.

   [hover a negative ion]

   Scissors on a negative ion does the reverse — takes the electrons back
   off and leaves you the atom.

   [hover a positive ion with electrons loose nearby]

   Plus makes a neutral atom. Give a positive ion the right number of
   electrons and it fuses. If they're loose elsewhere on the board, it
   will fly them in for you.

   [hover a carbonate]

   Some ions decompose. Carbonate breaks into an oxide ion and carbon
   dioxide. The button only appears on ions that genuinely do this, so
   nobody is guessing.

   [hover the H+/OH- pair, then the oxygen-carbon-oxygen from earlier]

   And the droplet. When the pieces you've assembled add up to a molecule,
   this offers to make it. Hydrogen and hydroxide give you water. Two
   H-plus and an oxide give you water. And the oxygen-carbon-oxygen I
   built a minute ago gives you carbon dioxide.

   [hover any piece on the left]

   One last thing. Go over to the reactants side and the tools are
   completely different — duplicate, delete, and that's it. You cannot
   create or destroy anything on the products side. Everything over there
   has to have come from something you already had.


5. THE TWO READOUTS  (45s)
--------------------------
[SHOW] Equation panel (right) and status strip (under the board).

- Equation panel logs every step as it happens, labelled: Formation,
  Bonding, Bond broken, Dissociation, Oxidation, Reduction, Half-equation,
  Decomposition, Condensation. It is a worked answer building itself.
- Status strip is the checklist:
    "Atoms C H2 Ca Cl2"      what must be accounted for
    "2 electrons still loose" park them somewhere
    "still charged: Cl-"      not finished
    "Products complete" tick  done


6. HOUSEKEEPING  (30s)
----------------------
- Drag on empty space to marquee-select; Shift-click to add; Esc to clear.
- Ctrl+D duplicates a reactant, Del removes one.
- Arrow button in the middle: reset products — wipes the right and copies
  the reactants across again. The undo button, effectively.


7. WORKED EXAMPLE  (90s)
------------------------
Do ONE end to end, narrating. Suggested: Acid + metal carbonate.

[SHOW] Load "Acid + metal carbonate (HCl + CaCO3)".
  1. Scissors the CaCO3 apart.
  2. Decompose the carbonate: CO3(2-) -> O(2-) + CO2.
  3. Scissors both acid pairs apart.
  4. Slide the two H+ onto the oxide's two sockets.
  5. Droplet: 2H+ + O(2-) -> H2O.
  6. Join Ca2+ with the two Cl- to finish CaCl2.
  Status strip goes green. Read the log back — it is the full equation.

If there is time, a second one with a different flavour: Metal displacement
(Zn + CuSO4) shows electrons moving between metals cleanly.


8. GOTCHAS TO MENTION  (30s)
----------------------------
- Water is deliberately two things: the H2O molecule, and the [H+][OH-] pair.
  The pair is for reactions that pull water apart; the droplet turns it back.
- The tool checks atoms and charge, not chemical sense. It will let a student
  build a molecule the reaction would not actually make. That judgement is
  still the teacher's — say so out loud.
- Aqueous electrolysis: nothing enforces which ion discharges. Good starting
  point, not a rule checker.


CLOSING (15s)
-------------
- One file, share it or host it, nothing to set up.
- Best used on a projector with students calling out the next move.
