Melting
Physics

Watch a snowman melt, and see where the energy comes from — mostly from the air, not the sunshine, because snow throws most of the light back. Then go inside the ice to watch the molecules break out of their pattern, while a heating curve tracks them and holds dead flat at 0 °C through the whole melt. Change the weather to change how long it takes.

Density
Physics

Spin 20 g of sugar into candy floss on a kitchen scale: the mass never changes, but the volume grows four hundred times. Zoom in to see why, weigh a slice of cake against a brownie the same size, then put the definition to work with cubes of wood, aluminium and iron — first the same volume, then the same mass. Finally, go inside the metals to see why iron's heavier atoms make it denser, compare solid, liquid and gas gold, and find out why ice floats when solid wax sinks.

Convection
Physics

Conduction
Physics
