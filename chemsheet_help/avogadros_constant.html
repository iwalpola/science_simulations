The big idea: a mole is just a pack size

In a shop, eggs come in boxes of 12. We call 12 things a dozen.

Chemists count atoms in packs too. But atoms are so tiny that the pack has to be enormous: 602,200,000,000,000,000,000,000 atoms. That pack is called a mole. The number in it, 6.022 × 1023, is the Avogadro constant.

If you counted one atom every second, it would take you about 19,000,000,000,000,000 years to count one mole. That's over a million times the age of the Universe.

Translate shop → chemistry

🛒 In the shop🧪 On the worksheet
one item (one egg)one atom
price of one itemmass of one atom

how many packs you buyhow many moles

There are only two moves

÷

You know the whole lot and want ONE.

A box of 12 costs £6.00, so one costs £6.00 ÷ 12.

×

You know ONE and want the whole lot.

One can costs 65p, so 24 cans cost 24 × 65p.

Step 0, every time: count the items.

number of items = number of packs × pack size

number of atoms = number of moles × 6.022 × 1023

Before you start: two calculator tips

Typing 6.022 × 1023. Use the ×10x button (on some calculators it's EXP or EE): type 6.022 ×10x 23.

⚠️ Dividing by it: if you type 12 ÷ 6.022 × 10^23, the calculator works out (12 ÷ 6.022) and then multiplies by 1023. That's completely wrong. Use the ×10x button, or brackets: 12 ÷ (6.022 × 10^23).

Significant figures (s.f.). Start counting at the first digit that isn't zero.

45.93 to 3 s.f. → 45.9  ·  0.000545 has 3 s.f.  ·  1.9927 × 10−23 to 3 s.f. → 1.99 × 10−23 (the ×10 part doesn't change).

To type an answer into the boxes on this page, write 1.99e-23 or 1.99x10^-23.

Question 1: know the whole lot, find ONE ÷ share out

1a÷

🛒 Shop: A box of 12 cupcakes costs £6.00. How much is one cupcake?

🧪 Worksheet: One dozen golf balls have a mass of 551.16 g. Calculate the mass of one golf ball (3 s.f.).

1b÷

🛒 Shop: 3 boxes of eggs (12 in each box) cost £5.40 altogether. How much is one egg?

🧪 Worksheet: Three dozen table tennis balls have a mass of 97.20 g. Calculate the mass of one table tennis ball (3 s.f.).

Watch out: don't just divide by 3. Three is the number of boxes. You need to share between all 36 items.

1c÷

Now the pack gets huge. Think of a bag of rice: lots of grains, so each grain costs almost nothing.

🛒 Shop: A bag of rice holds 50,000 grains and costs £1.20. How much does one grain cost?

🧪 Worksheet: One mole of 12 C atoms has a mass of 12.000 g. Calculate the mass of one 12 C atom (3 s.f.).

A huge pack means a tiny amount for each item. Your answer should be a very small number, with a big negative power of ten.

1d÷

🛒 Shop: 5 bags of rice (50,000 grains in each) cost £6.00 altogether. How much does one grain cost?

🧪 Worksheet: 5.0 moles of 197 Au atoms have a mass of 984.8 g. Calculate the mass of one 197 Au atom (3 s.f.).

Another way that also works: find the price of one bag first (£6.00 ÷ 5), then share that between the grains. In chemistry: 984.8 ÷ 5.0 = 196.96 g for one mole, then ÷ 6.022 × 1023.

Question 2: know ONE, find the whole lot × multiply

Now it's the other way round. You know the price of one item and want the price of many, so you multiply.

2a×

🛒 Shop: One can of cola costs 65p. How much do two dozen cans cost?

🧪 Worksheet: One cricket ball has a mass of 162.0 g. Calculate the mass of two dozen cricket balls (4 s.f.).

2b×

🛒 Shop: One grain of rice costs £0.000024. How much do 3 bags cost (50,000 grains in each)?

🧪 Worksheet: One 63 Cu atom has a mass of 1.045 × 10−22 g. Calculate the mass of 3.0 moles of 63 Cu atoms (4 s.f.).

2c×

You don't have to buy whole packs. Moles don't have to be whole numbers either.

🛒 Shop: One grain of rice costs £0.000024. You buy a small scoop that is 0.025 of a bag. How much does the scoop cost?

🧪 Worksheet: One 109 Ag atom has a mass of 1.808 × 10−22 g. Calculate the mass of 0.02500 moles of 109 Ag atoms (4 s.f.).

Question 3: a shopping basket + add up the bill

An atom is like a basket with three kinds of item in it. Here are the "prices", which are really the masses from the worksheet table:

🛒 Basket item🧪 Particle and mass
🥪 sandwich: £1.673proton: 1.673 × 10−24 g
🧃 drink: £1.675neutron: 1.675 × 10−24 g
🍬 sweet: £0.000911 (almost free!)electron: 9.110 × 10−28 g

These are silly prices. I copied the digits straight from the table so you can see it really is the same sum.

3a

🧪 Worksheet: How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in an atom of 73Li?

Think of the symbol as an order slip: 73Li

  • Bottom number (3) is the atomic number. It tells you the protons (🥪 sandwiches).
  • Top number (7) is the mass number. It counts the heavy things, protons + neutrons (🥪 + 🧃). The sweets are too light to count.
  • Meal deal rule: one sweet with every sandwich. An atom has no overall charge, so electrons = protons.
3b+

🛒 Shop: Put 3 sandwiches, 4 drinks and 3 sweets in a basket. What is the bill?

🧪 Worksheet: Calculate the mass of one atom of 73Li using your answer to (a) (4 s.f.).

The electrons barely change the answer, just like the sweets barely change the bill.

3c×

🛒 Shop: One basket costs £11.72. A school orders a crate of 30 identical baskets. What is the total?

🧪 Worksheet: Calculate the mass of one mole of 73Li atoms using your answer to (b) and the Avogadro constant (4 s.f.).

Your answer is almost exactly 7, the mass number. That's not a coincidence. See the pattern at the end.

Question 4: compare prices ÷ relative

"Relative to a proton" means "how many protons is it worth?" In the shop you'd ask "how many sandwiches is it worth?" You divide by the price of a sandwich.

The units cancel (£ ÷ £, or g ÷ g), so relative masses have no units.

4neutron

🛒 Shop: A sandwich costs £1.673 and a drink costs £1.675. How many sandwiches is a drink worth?

🧪 Worksheet: Find the mass of a neutron relative to the mass of one proton (3 s.f.).

4electron

🛒 Shop: A sweet costs £0.000911. How many sandwiches is a sweet worth? (And how many sweets would it take to pay for one sandwich?)

🧪 Worksheet: Find the mass of an electron relative to the mass of one proton (3 s.f.).

Spot the pattern: why such a weird pack size?

Look at the mass of one mole of each atom from your answers:

Atom (mass number)Mass of 1 mole
12 C (12)12.000 g (Q1c)
7 Li (7)7.058 g (Q3c)
63 Cu (63)188.8 ÷ 3.0 = 62.9 g (Q2b)
109 Ag (109)2.722 ÷ 0.02500 = 108.9 g (Q2c)
197 Au (197)984.8 ÷ 5.0 = 196.96 g (Q1d)

The pack size 6.022 × 1023 was chosen on purpose, so that one mole of atoms has a mass in grams about equal to its mass number. It's like a shop picking a pack size so that the price of the pack in pounds matches the number printed on each item. That turns tiny, awkward numbers into friendly ones.

Cheat sheet

  • Count the items first: atoms = moles × 6.022 × 1023 (just like items = packs × pack size).
  • Whole lot → one: divide. Mass of one atom = total mass ÷ number of atoms.
  • One → whole lot: multiply. Total mass = mass of one atom × number of atoms.
  • Mass of one atom: add up (number × mass) for protons, neutrons and electrons, like adding up a bill.
  • Relative mass: divide by the mass of a proton. No units.
  • Use the ×10x button, and give the number of s.f. the question asks for.
