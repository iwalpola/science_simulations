1. The periodic table: two numbers, and only one of them is mass

Every box on the periodic table carries the same two numbers. Click any element below and look at what changes.

The one rule to remember: the bigger number is the relative atomic mass, Ar. That is the one you use in every calculation on this sheet.

The smaller number is the atomic number — how many protons. You need it for electronic structure, not for mass.

Two elements where getting this wrong really shows: potassium is Ar 39 (atomic number 19), and copper is Ar 63.5 (atomic number 29). If you write 19 or 29, you have read the wrong number.

What "relative" means

Ar has no units. It is a comparison: a carbon-12 atom is called exactly 12, and every other atom is measured against it. So Ar = 24 for magnesium means a magnesium atom is twice as heavy as a carbon atom. That is all.

A few values aren't whole numbers — chlorine is 35.5, copper 63.5. That is because a real sample is a mixture of isotopes, and the number on the table is the average. Use 35.5 exactly as it is written; do not round it to 35 or 36.

2. Reading a formula: counting the atoms

Before you can add up any masses, you have to know how many of each atom there are. Three rules cover everything at GCSE.

What you seeWhat it means
H2OThe small number applies only to the symbol in front of it. 2 hydrogen, 1 oxygen. No number written means 1.
Ca(NO3)2The number outside the bracket multiplies everything inside. 1 calcium, 2 nitrogen, 6 oxygen (2 × 3).
2Al2O3A big number in front is how many of that whole thing there are in the equation. It is not part of the formula. The formula is still Al2O3.

This is the trap the whole sheet is built around. In 4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3, the formula of aluminium is Al, not Al4. The formula of oxygen is O2, not O6. The 4 and the 3 are balancing numbers, and they never go into an Mr.

Say it like a recipe: "4 lots of Al react with 3 lots of O2 to make 2 lots of Al2O3." The thing after "lots of" is the formula.

Elements that go around in pairs

Seven elements are written as pairs when they are on their own. You will meet them constantly:

H2   N2   O2   F2   Cl2   Br2   I2

Everything else — the metals, carbon, sulfur at GCSE — is written as a single symbol: Al, Na, Zn, Fe, Ti, C.

3. Working out Mr: four columns, every time

Relative formula mass (Mr) is just all the Ar values in a formula added together. Set it out the same way every single time and you will not lose track:

Worked example: Mr of Ca(NO3)2

Add, never multiply. Mr of H2O is 1 + 1 + 16 = 18. It is not 1 × 16.

Multiply inside a row, add down the last column. That is the only arithmetic in the whole topic.

Mr has no units. Don't write grams. (Grams appear the moment you go on to moles — but not here.)

Questions a–c: give the formula of each substance

These have no arithmetic at all. The whole task is to look at the equation and separate the balancing number from the formula.

a

4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3

b

ZnF2 + 2Na → 2NaF + Zn

Sodium is a metal, so it is just Na — the 2 in front is a balancing number. And zinc on the right is Zn for the same reason.

c

Fe2O3 + 3C → 2Fe + 3CO

Carbon monoxide is CO: one carbon, one oxygen. The 3 in front says there are three of them. Notice that CO and C are different substances — the balancing number never changes what something is.

Questions d–f: formula and Mr

Same first step as above, then feed each formula through the four-column table. Press Show the Mr working if you want to see every line.

d

2Mg + O2 → 2MgO

Magnesium's Mr is just its Ar, because the formula is a single atom. That is fine and it is not a trick.

e

2Fe + 3Cl2 → 2FeCl3

Chlorine is 35.5, so chlorine gas Cl2 is 71 and FeCl3 is 56 + 106.5. Decimals are meant to be there — don't round them away part-way through.

f

TiCl4 + 4Na → Ti + 4NaCl

Two chlorine compounds in one equation, with different numbers of chlorines. TiCl4 has 4 × 35.5 = 142 of chlorine; NaCl has only one.

Cheat sheet

  • Read the bigger number. Ar is the big one; the atomic number is the small one.
  • A big number in front is a balancing number. Cross it out before you write the formula. 2Al2O3 → the formula is Al2O3.
  • A small number applies to the symbol just before it. A number outside a bracket multiplies everything inside.
  • Mr = add up every Ar in the formula. Multiply across each row, add down the column.
  • H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2, I2 go around in pairs. Metals and carbon do not.
  • No units on Mr.
